# The First 7 Days — An Inheritance Walkthrough
**Bitcoin Custody Guardian · Draft v0.1 · 2026-10-06**

The heart of the app. This walkthrough is for someone who knows nothing about
Bitcoin and just lost someone who held it. It is written to be read straight
through, one day at a time — and its first rule outranks everything else:

> **Rule #1 — Nothing is urgent.** Bitcoin cannot be stolen by waiting. It
> *can* be lost by rushing. Every irreversible mistake in Bitcoin happens in
> minutes; every safe step can be taken calmly. You have time. Use it.

Read this once today, then do only Day 1. Come back tomorrow.

---

## Before you start — 5 sentences you need

1. **Bitcoin is digital money that lives on a public ledger.** It is not "in"
   any device, app, or account — all of those are *keys* that control it.
2. **Keys are what matter.** A **seed phrase** (12 or 24 common words) is the
   master key. Anyone who reads it can take everything. Nothing else in the
   setup matters without it.
3. **Hardware wallets** are small devices (Ledger, BitBox02, Jade, Trezor,
   Coldcard, SeedSigner, Bitkey) that hold keys safely. They are *safe to
   hold*. They are dangerous to reset.
4. **There is no customer support for lost keys.** No company, developer, or
   hacker can recover a lost seed phrase. That is why we are careful.
5. **People will offer to help.** Almost every stranger who offers to help a
   grieving heir with crypto is a scammer. This app is private and asks you
   for nothing. Trust caution first.

## Day 1 — Secure the paper, touch nothing

**Goal today: lose nothing. Recover nothing. Change nothing.**

DO:
- Collect everything Bitcoin-related into **one box** you control:
  - Any hardware wallets, USB sticks, cards with a chip (Ledger, BitBox02,
    Jade, Trezor, Coldcard, Bitkey)
  - Any papers with handwritten or printed **lists of words** (12 or 24, or
    numbered groups — sometimes split across pages)
  - Any "wallet files", USB drives labeled with wallet names, letters of
    instruction, envelopes with instructions
  - Anything metal with words/letters stamped or punched into it
- Keep the box somewhere safe and normal (a lockable drawer, a safe).
- Write down (on paper, not in the app) *where you found each thing*.

DON'T:
- ❌ Do not plug anything in yet.
- ❌ Do not reset, format, "clean up", or wipe any device — even if it seems
  locked or broken.
- ❌ Do not photograph word lists. Phones automatically back up photos to the
  cloud, and a photo of a seed phrase is a permanent leak.
- ❌ Do not type the words into any app, website, or this app.
- ❌ Do not delete anything on his computer or phone — "wallet" files,
  "Ledger Live", "Sparrow", "Electrum" — nothing.
- ❌ Do not tell anyone what you found or how much there is.

**How you should feel after today:** organized. Nothing is lost. Nothing is
at risk. That is a full day's work.

## Day 2 — Make a written inventory (no secrets on the page)

**Goal today: know what you have, in plain words, on your own paper.**

For each item in the box, write one line:
- What it physically is ("small black device, buttons, 'Ledger' on back")
- Where it was found ("second drawer of his desk")
- Anything written on it or its packaging ("label says 'main'")

Also walk the house for things that aren't in the box yet:
- Safe deposit box? Home safe? Attorney's office? Bank vault?
- A laptop he used alone (check browser bookmarks later — Day 4)
- An app on his phone: "Sparrow", "BlueWallet", "Muun", "Nunchuk", "River",
  "Strike", "Cash App", "Coinbase" — list the names, open nothing yet.

For word lists: **do not copy the words into your inventory.** Write only:
"24-word list found, sealed envelope, 3 pages."

**Today you may also check:** any mail/emails about Bitcoin tax forms,
statements, or exchange accounts. List the company names you see. These are
records, not keys — they are fine to read and keep.

## Day 3 — Learn what each thing is (the app explains, the box stays closed)

**Goal today: vocabulary. Not action.**

Open the app's Learn mode and read the short guides for whatever Day 2
found. Typical reading for an heir:
- "What is a seed phrase?" (5 min)
- "What is a hardware wallet?" (5 min)
- "What is Sparrow / Ledger Live / an exchange account?" (5 min each)
- "What is multisig? What is a 'descriptor' file?" (10 min — only if you
  found 2+ hardware wallets or a file mentioning '2 of 3')

You are building just enough understanding to make Day 4 and Day 5 safe.
If any sentence confuses you, ask the app to re-explain it simpler. That is
exactly what it is for.

## Day 4 — Look, don't touch (verification with open eyes)

**Goal today: understand the setup's *shape* without entering any secrets
anywhere.**

Safe actions today:
- **Word lists:** confirm each list is complete (the number of words it
  claims — usually 24). Look for extra instructions on the paper ("add this
  word when restoring" = a **passphrase**; note that it exists, do not
  combine it with anything yet).
- **Devices:** note their brand and model from the packaging or the back of
  the device. Do not power them on yet. (Some devices erase after wrong PIN
  attempts — you will power them on only when you have both the PIN *and*
  the backup words, later.)
- **His computer:** open his browser bookmarks. Wallet-related bookmarks are
  safe to *look at* (websites like sparrowwallet.com, ledger.com). Do not
  log into anything; just note the names.
- **Exchange accounts (River, Strike, Swan, Coinbase…):** you can see
  whether an account exists without touching funds. Do **not** try to reset
  passwords today — account recovery is a legal/identity process you will
  do deliberately in Day 5–6 with documents in hand.

**Today you are answering:** "Is this a device-and-words setup, a
device-and-words setup with an extra passphrase, a multisig setup with
several devices, and/or does he also have accounts at companies?" Most
setups are one of those four shapes.

## Day 5 — Know your options (no action yet)

**Goal today: understand the three honest paths forward.**

Path A — **Keep it in self-custody (heirs' default):**
The keys already work; nothing needs to move. In the coming weeks you will
learn to open the wallet on a clean computer, verify you can see the balance
(*watch-only* first), and only then decide anything. Slow, safe, and the
money never passes through anyone's hands.

Path B — **Move to a temporary custodian while you decide:**
Some heirs want funds somewhere simpler *while the estate is sorted*. If you
go this way, the corpus directory names Bitcoin-only companies with strong
customer service, proof of reserves, and long track records — and states
their criticisms honestly (custodial exchanges can freeze accounts during
compliance reviews; nothing there is "yours" in the self-custody sense).
Moving funds means the keys *were* used, so this is only done once you have
practiced recovering the wallet yourself. Never because a stranger suggested
it.

Path C — **Get professional help for complex estates:**
Large amounts, multiple keys with missing pieces, business entities, or any
legal complication → a **Bitcoin-knowledgeable estate attorney** (not a
"recovery expert") and possibly a collaborative-custody provider with a
documented inheritance process. The directory lists categories and
evidence; the app never picks a paid professional for you.

**Today's decision is not "which path" — it is only "which path do I want to
learn more about."** The app has deep-dive guides for each.

## Day 6 — Assemble your verification plan

**Goal today: a written plan for the *first real action*, which is always
the same: prove you can open the wallet without risking anything.**

Your plan will include:
1. **A clean computer** (his old laptop, wiped/reinstalled, or your own
   device used offline) — the app's guide explains what "clean" means and
   why.
2. **The wallet software** — downloaded only from the official site, with
   the checksum verification the guide walks you through (this is where the
   app earns its keep — verification is mechanical and safe).
3. **The order of operations** — the non-negotiable sequence:
   1. Install software → 2. Verify download → 3. Enter the seed words ONCE
      (into the verified software only) → 4. Write down the first receiving
      address → 5. Confirm the balance appears (or ask the app why it
      doesn't) → 6. **Nothing else.** No sending, no "upgrading", no
      swapping wallets the same day.
4. **A helper rule:** one difficult thing per day. Two in one day is how
   mistakes happen.

## Day 7 — Rest, then repeat what worked

**Goal today: consolidate.**

- Re-read your inventory and your Day 6 plan.
- Do **nothing irreversible.** If you completed Day 6's verification and saw
  the balance, you have already done the hardest part — and it was done
  *without spending anything, without sharing anything, and without trusting
  anyone.*
- Start the next phase at your pace: the app's guides on
  "spending safely", "moving to your own custody", "tax basics", and the
  directory are there when you're ready.

## The scam-defense card (read any day you're contacted)

If anyone — email, phone, forum, "friend of a friend" — offers help with
inherited crypto, it is a scam if ANY of these are true:

- They ask you to share, read aloud, or "verify" a seed phrase, passphrase,
  or any key material. **No legitimate party ever needs your words.**
- They ask you to install remote-control software (AnyDesk, TeamViewer,
  "support tool").
- They urge speed: "your funds are at risk", "the deadline is today".
- They ask you to "send a small amount first to unlock/test/verify".
- They contacted YOU first.

The safe answer is always: stop contact, do nothing, ask the app.
You lose nothing by ignoring every such message. The app's Scam-Checker
mode will analyze any message you paste — fully offline.

## For the app developer (design notes, not user-facing)

- Each "Day" maps to one guided mode with an explicit completion state the
  user can defer — never forced sequence, always available.
- The Day-6 verification sequence is the single most safety-critical
  content in the corpus: it must cite the verification/checksum procedure
  for each wallet software in T04 and flag "do these in one sitting" as
  danger-class.
- "Do not reset the device" and "never share words" repeat in every mode —
  guardrails enforce these phrases' presence near related topics.
- Word-completeness check (24 vs 12 words, split pages, SLIP-39 "numbered
  groups") belongs in Day 4 but must never ask the user to *type* the words
  — count them on paper.
- Scam card is a standalone component reachable from every screen (one tap),
  matching T08 content.
- Every day ends with a short "what you accomplished" recap the user can
  export as encrypted notes (opt-in) or just read — no analytics, no
  streaks, no gamification. Grieving users don't need streaks.
